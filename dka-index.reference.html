FLUIDS POTASSIUM INSULIN

Adult DKA / HHS

Overview, not an order set. Your DKA protocol wins where this disagrees.
Confirm & start fluids
  • CONFIRM DKA — glucose > 250, pH < 7.3 or HCO3 < 18, ketones present.
  • SUSPECT HHS instead — glucose often > 600, minimal ketosis, osmolality > 320.
  • GIVE 1 L balanced crystalloid (LR) — first hour.
  • TITRATE 250–500 mL/h to perfusion.
  • FIND the trigger — infection, ischemia, missed insulin, pump failure.
Potassium rules
  • K < 3.3 → HOLD insulin. REPLACE potassium first, 20–40 mEq/h with monitoring.
  • K 3.3–5.2 → ADD 20–30 mEq potassium per litre. START insulin.
  • K > 5.2 → START insulin, no potassium yet. RECHECK K every 2 h.
Insulin before the potassium is known is the error that kills.
Insulin & the finish line
  • START regular insulin 0.1 u/kg/h IV infusion. Bolus optional.
  • TITRATE to a glucose fall of 50–75 mg/dL/h.
  • Glucose < 250 → ADD D5 to the fluids. DO NOT stop the insulin.
  • STOP only once the gap is closed and the patient is eating.
  • OVERLAP subcutaneous long-acting by 2 h.
  • HHS → CORRECT slower. REPLACE a bigger fluid debt. SUSPECT thrombosis.
  • Headache or altered mental status during treatment → SUSPECT cerebral edema.
Sources: ADA/EASD consensus on the management of hyperglycemic crises in adults (Diabetes Care 2024) and the preceding ADA consensus statement (Diabetes Care 2009;32:1335); SQuID protocol context (Ann Emerg Med 2023) for mild DKA. — Personal/unofficial aid, not part of the hospital IT system/EHR, not a substitute for clinical judgment.
