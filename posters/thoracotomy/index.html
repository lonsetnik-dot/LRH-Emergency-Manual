Resuscitative Thoracotomy (Clamshell)

INDICATIONUnstable patient in extremis with deteriorating physiology and PROVEN cardiac tamponade on ultrasound. No signs of life + PEA on arrival = consistently poor (Alfred 2008–13: 9 independent survivors of 27).

EQUIPMENT
TRAUMA CART · THORACOSTOMY + RIB SPREADER shelves
(scalpel in SCALPEL drawer)
  • Scalpel — large blade, No. 10 / 20
  • Heavy Mayo scissors / trauma shears
  • Finochietto rib spreader — RIB SPREADER shelf
  • Gigli saw or heavy scissors — for the sternum
  • Artery forceps (pericardium) + long forceps
  • 3-0 nylon or prolene suture on large needle · skin stapler · Foley catheter (wound occlusion)
  • Vascular / aortic cross-clamp · internal defib paddles
  • Chlorhexidine or iodine — splash prep only
IN PARALLEL — OTHER HANDS
  • ETT in place · bilateral finger thoracostomies
  • Large-bore access (14 g / RIC / MAC) · blood + MTP
FIG 1 · CLAMSHELL INCISION — 5TH ICS, AXILLA TO AXILLA
STEPS
  1. Gate the decision: tamponade proven on ultrasound — it may hide with no external signs and a normal ECG. Airway, bilateral finger thoracostomies, and MTP run in parallel.
  2. Splash prep — no delay for full asepsis.
  3. Extend the LEFT thoracostomy incision along the 5th ICS, sternum → mid-axillary line.
  4. Cut intercostal muscle into pleura along the full length with heavy scissors.
  5. Mirror the incision on the RIGHT; divide the sternum transversely — Gigli saw or heavy scissors.
  6. Finochietto in, handle toward the axilla; open WIDE; sweep the left lung out of the way.
  7. Tent the pericardium with artery forceps; open with a long VERTICAL incision, anterior to the phrenic nerve.
  8. Deliver the heart; evacuate clot; inspect every surface for the wound.
  9. Wound control: finger occlusion first → staples or 3-0 nylon/prolene suture (avoid coronaries) → Foley balloon through the defect, gentle traction.
  10. Internal massage, two-handed, ~100/min; VF → internal defibrillation 10–20 J.
  11. Aorta: hand along the posterior left chest wall — compress the descending aorta against the spine or cross-clamp at the diaphragm.
  12. ROSC → mammary + intercostal bleeding WILL start — control it; sedate/ventilate; straight to OR / transfer.
LOCALIZE: agree in advance whether ED thoracotomy is in this facility’s scope and what surgical backup exists — decide before the event, not at the bedside.
