Simulations — the patient is a pillow

Ten-to-fifteen-minute low-fidelity drills that chain the manual's real workflows together — birth into dystocia into a blue baby, sepsis into pressors into a transfer call. One person reads the script; the recorder drives the actual cards. No mannequin required. Every drill ends with the localization gaps it tends to surface — the local facts nobody thought to write down until a workflow stalled on them. See ONBOARDING.md.

⚠ IN-SITU SIM SAFETY: announce "this is a simulation" at start and end, and post someone to catch real patients/calls. Never open real medications or sterile kits — sim props stay labeled SIM and never enter real stock. A real clinical need ends the sim immediately. Nothing about a real patient goes on the whiteboard or into the tools.
HOW TO RUN A PILLOW SIM
  • Cast: 3–6 people. Minimum: a leader, a recorder (drives the manual — that's the skill being drilled), and a facilitator who reads the script and voices the monitor ("sat is 82"). Add hands, meds, and a family actor as available.
  • Stage: pillow in a gown on a stretcher. A rolled towel in a pillowcase is the baby. Vitals live on a whiteboard or the facilitator's voice. Point at where you'd push, cut, or place — say the dose out loud, give nothing.
  • Advance on actions, not time: each phase ends when the team does the things under ADVANCE WHEN. If they stall, the facilitator drops a hint ("what does the card say comes next?"), not the answer.
  • Recorder really uses the tools: open the linked card, enter the (fictional) weight, start the timers, tap the dose buttons. Half the value of these drills is fluency with the manual itself. RESET FOR NEXT CASE when the sim ends.
  • Always debrief — five minutes, same shift, using the Debrief card. Plus/delta/plus; one or two takeaways, owners named.

S1Precipitous Birth → Shoulder Dystocia → Blue Baby

OB SET · 15 MIN + DEBRIEF
IMMINENT DELIVERY→SHOULDER DYSTOCIA→NEONATAL RESUSCITATION
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Pillow-mom in a gown on the stretcher, knees-up. Towel-roll baby hidden under the gown. Whiteboard: G3P2 at term, "the baby is coming," 5 minutes out the door.
  • Roles: leader/deliverer · recorder (drives the OB tool) · nurse · facilitator (voices mom + vitals). Family actor optional.
  • Walk to the real OB cart — point at (don't open) the vaginal-delivery drawer and the airway/breathing drawer, so hands know where things live.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow + gown (mom) · rolled towel in a pillowcase (baby) · whiteboard + marker · one dry towel and a hat for the "baby" (these two may be real — they touch nothing sterile).
  • OB / Neonatal Cart · VAGINAL DELIVERY drawer: point, don't open — clamps, cord scissors, bulb suction live here.
  • OB / Neonatal Cart · AIRWAY / BREATHING drawer: point — infant BVM + masks. Stage a separate SIM-labeled infant BVM if you want hands-on PPV.
  • Radiant warmer: turn it on for real — warm-up time is part of the drill.
PHASE 1 · IMMINENT DELIVERY
"She's pushing. You can see hair at the introitus. She screams — it's coming NOW."
OPEN › OB · IMMINENT NORMAL DELIVERY
  • Recorder opens the card, stamps time of birth when the facilitator calls it. Team calls OB + nursery, warms the "baby," assigns who catches.
ADVANCE WHEN › delivery roles are claimed and the card is open with the clock ready.
PHASE 2 · CURVEBALL — THE HEAD IS OUT, NOTHING ELSE
"The head delivered a minute ago and retracted back against the perineum — turtle sign. The shoulders will not come with normal traction."
OPEN › OB · SHOULDER DYSTOCIA
  • Team says it out loud, calls for help, and works the maneuvers in order on the pillow — legs back (McRoberts), suprapubic pressure (someone actually places hands and says where and which direction).
  • Facilitator releases the "baby" after two maneuvers done correctly and narrated.
ADVANCE WHEN › two maneuvers are performed and narrated correctly.
PHASE 3 · THE BABY IS OUT — AND BLUE
"Baby's out — floppy, blue, not crying. Cord is cut. What do you do, and where?"
OPEN › OB · NEONATAL RESUSCITATION
  • Towel-roll baby to the warmer. Warm–dry–stimulate, then PPV narrated at the right rate (use the card's metronome). Facilitator gives HR by voice: "HR 50… now 90 with good PPV."
  • Recorder logs 1- and 5-minute marks from the time-of-birth clock stamped in Phase 1 — that's the payoff of stamping it early.
END WHEN › HR > 100 narrated, baby "pinking up," 5-minute mark logged.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • Time of birth stamped at delivery, not reconstructed later. Dystocia named out loud within seconds of the turtle sign. Nobody pulls harder — they change geometry.
  • The baby moves to the warmer with a named person, and PPV starts inside a minute of "not crying."
  • Debrief card prompts: where did the extra hands come from at the dystocia call — and was that the fastest path? · who watched mom while everyone watched the baby?
  • Runs on a table as a VEMS deck when you cannot get the room or the people.

S2Delivery → Postpartum Hemorrhage → MTP

OB SET · 12 MIN + DEBRIEF
DELIVERY DONE→PPH→MASSIVE TRANSFUSION
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Pillow-mom post-delivery, placenta "out 10 minutes ago." A red towel or sheet under her is the growing puddle. Whiteboard: BP 112/70 → will fall.
  • Roles: leader · recorder (OB tool + Codes MTP card) · meds voice · facilitator. The facilitator adds "more blood on the sheet" every 90 seconds until the team escalates correctly.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow-mom · red towel/sheet (the growing puddle) · whiteboard for vitals + running EBL.
  • OB / Neonatal Cart · POSTPARTUM HEMORRHAGE drawer: point, don't open — uterotonics are named aloud, never handled.
  • Blood bank: the real MTP activation number, written where the team can read it (say it, don't dial).
  • JADA / balloon kit: point at where your department actually stocks it (see the labels tool).
PHASE 1 · IT'S NOT STOPPING
"The nurse calls you back in: 'she's soaking through pads.' Fundus feels boggy. Estimated loss so far: 700 mL and climbing."
OPEN › OB · POSTPARTUM HEMORRHAGE
  • Team works the card in order and says each dose out loud: uterine massage (hands on pillow, narrated), oxytocin, TXA with the time window stated, second IV, call OB.
ADVANCE WHEN › massage + two meds verbalized with doses, second IV named.
PHASE 2 · CURVEBALL — SHE'S CRASHING
"Loss is past 1.5 L. BP 78/40, HR 132, she's pale and confused. Uterotonics are in. What now, and who are you calling?"
OPEN › CODES · MASSIVE TRANSFUSION PROTOCOL
  • Someone activates MTP by its actual phone number / process (say it, don't dial). Recorder opens the MTP card and assigns a blood runner and a checker.
  • Bonus branch if time allows: leader states the next mechanical step from the PPH card (balloon / JADA) and where that kit lives.
END WHEN › MTP activation is verbalized end-to-end and roles for blood are named.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • Escalation happens on the trajectory (climbing loss + falling BP), not after a magic number. TXA's window is said out loud. The MTP call is a sentence, not a debate.
  • Debrief card prompts: what number or trigger would have made us call MTP one loop earlier? · did the recorder's card-driving keep pace with the room?
  • Runs on a table as a VEMS deck when you cannot get the room or the people.

S3Pediatric Asthma → Arrest → Post-ROSC

PEDS SET · 15 MIN + DEBRIEF
SEVERE ASTHMA→ARREST (PEDS MODE)→POST-ROSC
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Small pillow = a 4-year-old, parent-reported 16 kg. Whiteboard: sat 84% on room air, silent chest. A parent actor at the bedside raises the difficulty nicely.
  • First move of the sim: recorder enters 16 kg in the Peds weight bar — every dose downstream, including in the arrest tool, hangs off it. That hand-off is the thing being drilled.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: small pillow (the 4-year-old) · whiteboard · a phone/tablet with the Peds tool open — the weight bar is the first prop used.
  • Pediatric (Broselow) Cart · WHITE drawer (15–18 kg): point, don't open — this is the drawer 16 kg maps to.
  • SIM-labeled pediatric BVM (or mime it) · defib pads mimed on the pillow.
  • Code Cart · MEDICATIONS drawer: point — epi is named with its weight-based dose, never drawn up.
PHASE 1 · SEVERE ASTHMA
"Four-year-old, third albuterol neb done, still sat 84, silent chest, sleepy. Parent: 'he's never been this bad.'"
OPEN › PEDS · ASTHMA EXACERBATION
  • Team escalates per the card and says the weight-based doses aloud (the card computes them from the 16 kg). Someone states what "getting worse" will look like and what the trigger to bag is.
ADVANCE WHEN › escalation doses verbalized from the card and the arrest trigger is stated.
PHASE 2 · CURVEBALL — HE STOPS
"He goes limp. No pulse. Monitor shows a slow wide rhythm, then nothing organized."
OPEN › ARREST ENGINE (CARRIES THE 16 KG ACROSS)
  • Compressions on the pillow at real rate. Recorder starts the arrest tool — confirm it shows the pediatric weight carried over — and taps the epi timer as the "dose" is announced.
  • Leader states the asthma-arrest specifics from the reference accordions: what to do about the vent/bagging (slow rate, watch for breath stacking).
ADVANCE WHEN › two epi cycles timed in the tool; facilitator then grants ROSC.
PHASE 3 · ROSC — NOW WHAT?
"Pulse is back, sat climbing. He's not waking up. The next ten minutes decide the next ten days."
OPEN › CODES · POST-ROSC CARE
  • Recorder opens Post-ROSC — the ROSC clock should already be running, handed across from the arrest tool. Team walks the checklist: pressure target, sat/CO2 window, temperature, who they're calling for transfer.
END WHEN › post-ROSC checklist walked and the transfer call is named.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • Weight entered once, early, and trusted everywhere after. Arrest recognized inside 10 seconds of the limp doll. Post-ROSC opened before anyone relaxes.
  • Debrief card prompts: did the tools' shared weight/clock hand-offs actually work for us? · who was talking to the parent during compressions?

S4Pediatric Status Epilepticus → RSI → Vent Crash (DOPE)

PEDS SET · 12 MIN + DEBRIEF
STATUS EPILEPTICUS→RSI→DOPE CRASH
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Pillow = a 6-year-old, ~20 kg, "seizing for 10 minutes, one dose of midazolam by EMS." Recorder enters the weight first.
  • Roles: leader · airway · meds voice · recorder · facilitator. Stage a real (empty, SIM-labeled) BVM if available; otherwise mime it.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow (the 6-year-old) · whiteboard · SIM-labeled BVM · working suction to point at.
  • Pediatric (Broselow) Cart · BLUE drawer (19–23 kg): point — 20 kg maps here; tube sizes live on the card.
  • Code Cart · INTUBATION + ETT / BOUGIE drawers: point — laryngoscopes, tubes, bougie.
  • Vent: if a real one is nearby, stand next to it for the DOPE phase — touch nothing.
PHASE 1 · STILL SEIZING
"Second benzo is in. Three minutes later he is still seizing. What's your second-line, at what dose, and how long does it take to run in?"
OPEN › PEDS · STATUS EPILEPTICUS
ADVANCE WHEN › second-line agent + weight-based dose + infusion time verbalized from the card.
PHASE 2 · SECURE THE AIRWAY
"He's not protecting his airway and the seizure is finally slowing. The room agrees: intubate."
OPEN › CODES · INTUBATION / RSI CHECKLIST
  • Run the RSI checklist out loud as a challenge-response: drugs with peds doses, tube size, backup plan. Airway "intubates" the pillow; recorder logs the time.
ADVANCE WHEN › checklist completed as challenge-response and tube "placed."
PHASE 3 · CURVEBALL — HE CRASHES ON THE VENT
"Five minutes later: sat 79 and falling, pressure alarm. He's intubated and sedated. Why — work it as a list, not a guess."
OPEN › CODES · UNSTABLE ON THE VENT — DOPE
  • Team walks D-O-P-E out loud, states the action for each letter, and the facilitator reveals the answer (a plugged tube — suction fixes it) only when the letter is actually reached in order.
END WHEN › DOPE walked in order and the plug found at its letter.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • Second-line is named without a phone-a-friend. RSI is a checklist, not a vibe. The desaturation triggers the mnemonic, not random tweaking.
  • Debrief card prompts: which RSI checklist line would we actually skip under pressure — and what would catch us?

S5STEMI → VF Arrest → PEA → Code Status → Debrief

MEDICAL SET · 18 MIN + DEBRIEF
STEMI→VF→PEA→CODE STATUS→DEBRIEF
SETUP · 3 MIN
  • Pillow = a 68-year-old with crushing chest pain. Print or pull up any old STEMI teaching EKG. A family actor is required for this one — that's the point of the chain.
  • Roles: leader · compressor(s) · meds/defib voice · recorder · family actor · facilitator.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow · any old STEMI teaching EKG (printed) · whiteboard · two chairs for the family conversation · a phone/tablet with the arrest tool open.
  • Code Cart · MEDICATIONS drawer: point — lytic checklist is read from the card, drugs stay shut.
  • Defibrillator: pads mimed on the pillow; shocks are called out loud with the tool's timer.
  • Transfer: the configured transfer number visible on the whiteboard (say it, don't dial).
PHASE 1 · STEMI
"EKG in hand: anterior STEMI. You are 90 minutes from a cath lab. Walk your card: who do you call, what does the patient get, what's the lytic decision?"
OPEN › CODES · STEMI
ADVANCE WHEN › transfer call placed (verbalized) and the lytic decision is stated with its checklist.
PHASE 2 · CURVEBALL — HE ARRESTS IN FRONT OF YOU
"Mid-sentence he slumps. Monitor: VF. Family is standing in the doorway."
OPEN › ARREST ENGINE — RUN THE VF LOOP
  • Compressions on the pillow, real rate. Shocks called with the tool; epi/amio timed on its buttons. After two shock loops the facilitator changes the story: "rhythm check — organized and slow, no pulse. PEA." Team switches branches in the tool and hunts causes (H's & T's) out loud.
  • Nobody sends the family away — someone is assigned to them. That assignment is a scored behavior in this sim.
ADVANCE WHEN › VF→PEA branch switch done in the tool and a family support person is named.
PHASE 3 · THE CONVERSATION, MID-CODE
"Twenty minutes in. The nurse says quietly: 'his daughter says he has a POLST at home — he never wanted this.' You're still coding him."
OPEN › CONVERSATIONS · CODE STATUS
  • Leader (or a designated second physician) has the conversation with the daughter while compressions continue — using the card's language. The team then acts on the answer: transition to comfort, stop compressions, note the time.
ADVANCE WHEN › the conversation happens in-role and an explicit stop decision is made and time-stamped.
PHASE 4 · DEBRIEF FOR REAL + WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE
  • Run the actual Debrief card as Phase 4, in-sim — state the Basic Assumption out loud, walk the timeline from the arrest tool's log, plus/delta/plus. This is the phase teams skip in real life; here it's scripted.
  • Good looks like: the STEMI transfer call already made (so the arrest doesn't orphan it), clean VF→PEA branch handling, family never abandoned, and the goals-of-care conversation changing what the team does.

S6Hyperkalemia → Unstable Bradycardia → Transvenous Pacing

MEDICAL SET · 12 MIN + DEBRIEF
HYPERKALEMIA→UNSTABLE BRADY→TVP
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Pillow = a dialysis patient who missed two sessions. Whiteboard: K 7.8, wide QRS. Stage near the TVP kit's real location (point, don't open).
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow (dialysis patient) · whiteboard (K 7.8, wide QRS) · a printed ugly-QRS strip if you have one.
  • Code Cart · MEDICATIONS drawer: point — calcium, insulin/dextrose, albuterol all named with doses, never drawn.
  • Pacing pads: mimed on the pillow; the generator settings are said out loud.
  • {{physical.resusRoom}} cabinets · TRANSVENOUS PACING kit: point, don't open — the walk to it is part of the drill.
PHASE 1 · CRASH THE POTASSIUM
"K is 7.8 with a wide, ugly QRS. Order the sequence — drug, dose, what it does, what it doesn't."
OPEN › CODES · HYPERKALEMIA CRASH PROTOCOL
ADVANCE WHEN › calcium-first sequence verbalized with doses and the "temporizing vs removing" distinction stated.
PHASE 2 · CURVEBALL — HE BRADYS DOWN
"Despite calcium: HR 28, BP 70/40, barely responsive. Atropine does nothing — as the card predicts. What's next, physically?"
OPEN › CODES · SYMPTOMATIC BRADYCARDIA
  • Pads on the pillow, transcutaneous pacing narrated — rate, output, and the capture check ("do we feel a pulse with each spike?").
ADVANCE WHEN › TC pacing set up and the capture question asked out loud.
PHASE 3 · FLOAT THE WIRE
"Transcutaneous captures intermittently and he's screaming with every spike. Dialysis is two hours away. Bridge him properly."
OPEN › PROCEDURES · TRANSVENOUS PACING
  • Walk the checklist on the pillow: access site, sheath, balloon up at the right depth, generator settings said out loud (rate / output / sensing), capture confirmed, balloon down, secure.
END WHEN › generator settings verbalized correctly and capture confirmed in narration.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • Calcium before anything shifty. Pacing decisions driven by perfusion, not the monitor number. The TVP checklist actually read, not recalled.
  • Debrief card prompt: who in the room has actually floated a wire — and how do the rest of us borrow that before the real one?

S9Angioedema → Failed Airway Ladder → FONA

MEDICAL SET · 15 MIN + DEBRIEF
ANGIOEDEMA→FAILED LADDER→SALAD→CICO → FONA
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Pillow = a 55-year-old on lisinopril, tongue and lips swelling for an hour, voice getting muffled. Stage near the airway cart; point at where the video laryngoscope, bougie, and the FONA kit actually live.
  • Roles: leader · airway operator · second operator (neck) · meds voice · recorder (drives the ladder) · facilitator. This one is about saying the next rung out loud before you need it.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow · whiteboard (sat trend) · a marker for the neck landmarks — marking the pillow's "neck" is encouraged.
  • Code Cart · VIDEO + DIRECT LARYNGOSCOPY and ETT / BOUGIE drawers: point — every ladder rung has a drawer.
  • Code Cart · SURGICAL / SUPRAGLOTTIC + iGEL + FRONT OF NECK ACCESS drawers: point — every person in the room should be able to find FONA blind.
  • {{physical.resusRoom}} cabinets · SALAD SUCTION: point — suction leads in the soiled-airway phase.
PHASE 1 · THE AIRWAY IS CLOSING
"Voice is muffled, he's drooling, tongue looks like a fist. You will not get a second look at this airway. Plan it out loud."
OPEN › CODES · ANGIOEDEMA
  • Meds verbalized per the card — and the team states plainly what meds will and won't do for bradykinin-mediated swelling. Double setup declared: who intubates, who owns the neck, where the FONA kit is physically.
ADVANCE WHEN › double setup assigned and the neck is marked (narrated) before any attempt.
PHASE 2 · WORK THE LADDER
"First look: all you see is swollen tissue. Sat 91 and drifting. Call the rung you're on and the next one."
OPEN › AIRWAY · FAILED INTUBATION LADDER
  • Recorder drives the ladder tool; the operator narrates each rung — optimize, change device/operator, supraglottic attempt — with the sat read out between rungs. The leader's job: keep the room ahead of the ladder, not behind it.
ADVANCE WHEN › two rungs attempted and the team states the CICO trigger unprompted.
PHASE 3 · CURVEBALL — BLOOD IN THE AIRWAY
"The last attempt made him bleed — the view is now blood and swelling. Sat 84."
OPEN › PROCEDURES · SALAD
  • One SALAD pass narrated on the pillow — suction leads, catheter parked, scope follows. If the view is still nothing, that is the answer: say it.
ADVANCE WHEN › SALAD narrated and the operator makes an explicit call: view or no view.
PHASE 4 · CICO — CUT THE NECK
"Can't intubate. Supraglottic won't seat past the swelling. Bag-mask barely moves air. Sat 78. Say the words."
OPEN › CODES · FRONT OF NECK ACCESS (FONA)
  • Someone declares "this is CICO — we are cutting" out loud, and the second operator (already on the neck since Phase 1) walks the card on the pillow: laryngeal handshake, vertical incision, finger, bougie, tube. Facilitator grants sats once the sequence is narrated correctly.
END WHEN › CICO declared in words and the FONA sequence narrated start to finish.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • The neck is owned and marked before the first look. Every rung is named before it's climbed. CICO is declared by its name — the failure mode this sim exists to kill is the room that keeps trying "one more look" at sat 70.
  • Debrief card prompts: what sat number or attempt count is OUR line in the sand — and did we say it before or after we crossed it? · could every person in the room find the FONA kit blind?

S11Sepsis → Septic Shock → Pressors → Disposition

MEDICAL · 15 MIN + DEBRIEF
RECOGNITION→BUNDLE→SHOCK→DISPOSITION
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Pillow patient on a stretcher, gowned. Whiteboard: 72-year-old, three days of cough, sent in by the nursing home. HR 118 · BP 88/50 · RR 26 · T 38.9 · SpO₂ 91% on room air.
  • Roles: leader · recorder (drives the card) · nurse (access, blood) · facilitator (voices vitals and the lab).
  • Run this one on a real clock. Sepsis is the drill where the finding is almost always a duration, not a decision — how long antibiotics actually took, not whether the team knew to give them.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow + gown · whiteboard · two SIM-labeled blood culture bottles · an empty fluid bag marked SIM.
  • Walk to the pressors. Point at where norepinephrine actually lives — and say out loud whether it is a premixed bag, a vial somebody has to mix, or a pharmacy call. That answer is the sim.
  • Point at the antibiotics. Which of them are in the automated cabinet on this floor, and which need pharmacy? Do not open anything.
PHASE 1 · RECOGNITION AND THE BUNDLE CLOCK
“She is hot, confused, and breathing fast. The nurse says the blood pressure has been in the 80s since triage — about forty minutes ago.”
OPEN › CODES · SEPSIS / SEPTIC SHOCK
  • Team names sepsis out loud and starts the clock. Cultures before antibiotics, lactate sent, access obtained, fluids started — all stated with who is doing each one.
  • Facilitator, time these for real: minutes from “sepsis” being said to antibiotics being ordered, and to antibiotics being hung. The gap between those two numbers is usually the finding.
ADVANCE WHEN › cultures, lactate, access and antibiotics are all assigned to named people.
PHASE 2 · CURVEBALL — FLUID DOES NOT FIX IT
“Thirty mL/kg is in. MAP is 54. Lactate came back at 5.8. She is more confused than she was.”
  • Team moves to vasopressors and says the target out loud. Recorder logs the time pressors were started, not ordered.
  • Ask the question that finds the gap: “Is this going through a peripheral line, and for how long is that acceptable here?” Then: “Who is putting in the central line, and how long until they are here?”
  • If a central line is the answer, the recorder opens it and the team says the site and the size.
    OPEN › PROCEDURES · CENTRAL VENOUS CATHETER
ADVANCE WHEN › a pressor is running, a MAP target is named, and the route is settled.
PHASE 3 · SOURCE AND DISPOSITION
“Chest film shows a dense right lower lobe. She is on 0.15 of norepinephrine. It is 2am. Where is this patient in an hour?”
  • Team decides: ICU here, or transfer. Say the actual answer for this department at this hour — not the daytime one.
  • If transfer: the call goes out now, while the resuscitation continues. Recorder logs who was called and when.
END WHEN › a disposition is named with a person attached to it, and the transfer call is either placed or explicitly not needed.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • Somebody says the word “sepsis” out loud in the first two minutes, and a clock starts when they do.
  • Antibiotics are hung, not just ordered. The team knows the difference and tracks the second number.
  • Pressors start when fluid fails, not after a second round of fluid that nobody expected to work.
  • Debrief card prompts: how long was it between deciding on antibiotics and them being in the patient — and where did that time go? · did anyone have to leave the room to find something?
⚠ LOCALIZATION GAPS THIS DRILL SURFACES

This is the drill's second job. Write down every moment somebody had to ask a question the manual should have answered — those moments are the backlog. The keys below are the ones this case usually exposes; the list is a starting point, not a checklist.

  • contacts.transferPrimary.* — if the answer was “transfer”, did anyone have to look up the number?
  • caps.criticalAccess · ICU capability — the honest 2am answer, not the policy one.
  • physical.resusRoom · physical.cartDrawers — anything anyone had to hunt for.
  • Not yet a config key: your pressor stock form (premixed vs mixed vs pharmacy), your lactate turnaround, and how long a peripheral pressor is acceptable here. If your team argued about any of those, that is a value worth adding to localization.manifest.mjs — see ONBOARDING.md, “If something is not localizable”.

S12Acute Stroke → Lytic Decision → Thrombectomy Transfer

MEDICAL · 15 MIN + DEBRIEF
LAST KNOWN WELL→IMAGING→LYTIC→TRANSFER
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Pillow patient. Whiteboard: 68-year-old, right-sided weakness and no speech. Husband is in the waiting room. BP 186/98 · glucose 7.2 · afebrile.
  • Roles: leader · recorder · nurse · facilitator (voices the husband, the scanner and the receiving center). The husband role matters — last known well only exists if somebody asks him properly.
  • Real clock. Door-to-needle is the whole point, and it is measured in minutes people can feel.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow + gown · whiteboard · a printed blank stroke scale · a phone that is actually in the room.
  • Walk the route to CT with a stretcher, at speed, and time it. Nobody knows this number until they measure it, and it is on the critical path of every stroke you will ever have.
  • Point at the lytic. Where is it kept, who mixes it, and does that person have to be called in? Do not open it.
PHASE 1 · LAST KNOWN WELL
“EMS says ‘found down, unclear onset.’ The husband is outside and has not been asked anything yet.”
OPEN › CODES · SUSPECTED ACUTE STROKE
  • Somebody is sent to the husband with the specific question — not “when did this start” but “when did you last see her completely normal?” Facilitator gives “she was fine when I brought her tea, about 55 minutes ago.”
  • Recorder stamps LKW. A stroke scale is done and the number said out loud.
ADVANCE WHEN › LKW is a real time on the record and a scale score has been stated.
PHASE 2 · IMAGING AND THE LYTIC DECISION
“CT: no hemorrhage. No other exclusions on the checklist. Blood pressure is still 186/98.”
  • Team works the inclusion/exclusion checklist on the card out loud, then names the lytic this department actually stocks and its dose. If the card shows a placeholder rather than an agent, stop and write that down — that is the finding.
  • Blood pressure is addressed before the lytic, and the target is stated.
  • Facilitator: log the door-to-needle time. Announce it at the debrief, not before.
ADVANCE WHEN › the lytic decision is made and the agent, dose and route are said out loud by name.
PHASE 3 · CURVEBALL — SHE IS A THROMBECTOMY CANDIDATE
“CTA shows a proximal M1 occlusion. The receiving center wants to know your last known well and your scale score — and asks whether you have given the lytic.”
  • The transfer call is placed from the room, on the card's tap-to-call link. If that link does not dial anything, that is the finding — write it down.
  • Team hands over in a structured way: LKW, scale, imaging, lytic given or not, and the time of each.
  • Somebody is assigned to the husband before the patient leaves.
END WHEN › the transfer is accepted, the mode of transport is named, and the handover has been given once, completely.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • Somebody went to the family in the first three minutes. LKW is a time, not “this morning”.
  • The scan happened while the checklist was being worked, not after it.
  • The transfer conversation started before the lytic was finished, not after.
  • Debrief card prompts: what was our door-to-needle, and which single minute is the easiest one to get back? · who spoke to the husband, and when?
⚠ LOCALIZATION GAPS THIS DRILL SURFACES

Write down every moment somebody had to ask a question the manual should have answered. The keys below are the ones this case usually exposes.

  • formulary.strokeLytic — the card must name your agent and dose. Tenecteplase and alteplase are dosed completely differently.
  • formulary.thrombectomyTrigger — what actually triggers the CTA and the call here.
  • contacts.transfer · contacts.transferPrimary.* — did the tap-to-call link work, and did it reach the right desk?
  • policy.stroke — does the card cite your protocol, or is it still citing somebody else's, or none?
  • Not yet a config key: your stretcher-to-CT time, who mixes the lytic out of hours, and whether your scanner is ever occupied. If any of those changed the outcome, they are worth writing into the card.

S13STEMI → Reperfusion Decision → Transfer Clock

MEDICAL · 12 MIN + DEBRIEF
EKG→ACTIVATION→REPERFUSION→TRANSFER
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Pillow patient. Whiteboard: 58-year-old, crushing chest pain for 40 minutes, diaphoretic. HR 92 · BP 132/84 · SpO₂ 96%.
  • Print or draw an EKG with inferior ST elevation and hand it over at the right moment. A card that says “the EKG shows STEMI” teaches nothing; a piece of paper somebody has to interpret does.
  • Roles: leader · recorder · nurse · facilitator (voices the receiving cardiologist, who will push back).
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow + gown · a printed EKG · whiteboard · a real phone.
  • Walk to wherever the STEMI or chest-pain bundle lives — a box in the automated cabinet, a shelf, or nowhere. Say out loud what is in it. Do not open it.
  • If this department gives a lytic for STEMI, point at it and name who mixes it.
PHASE 1 · EKG TO ACTIVATION
“Triage hands you this EKG. It was taken four minutes ago.” (Hand over the paper.)
OPEN › CODES · STEMI
  • Read the top of the card first. It states whether this department has on-site PCI — and therefore whether this card is even the right pathway. If it says nobody has recorded that yet, stop the sim and write it down. That single unanswered boolean is the whole reperfusion strategy.
  • Team activates. Who is paged, by whom, on what number? Say it, then actually do it as far as is safe.
  • Recorder stamps EKG time and activation time.
ADVANCE WHEN › activation has happened and both times are on the record.
PHASE 2 · THE REPERFUSION DECISION
“The receiving center says: ‘Our lab is 90 minutes away by ground and the helicopter is weathered out.’ What are you doing?”
  • The team makes the PCI-versus-lytic call explicitly and says the reasoning out loud, including the time estimate it depends on.
  • If a lytic: name the agent, the dose, the contraindication check, and who is drawing it up. If PCI: name the transport and the time it will actually take at this hour.
  • Antiplatelet and anticoagulant decisions are said out loud, with doses.
ADVANCE WHEN › a reperfusion strategy is chosen, justified, and being executed by a named person.
PHASE 3 · CURVEBALL — HE ARRESTS IN THE BAY
“Mid-conversation he goes stiff and stops responding. Monitor shows VF.”
OPEN › ARREST ENGINE
  • Recorder switches to the arrest engine and reads the first shock energy off the screen. If it shows a placeholder instead of a number, the defibrillator has not been configured — write that down, it is the highest-value finding available in any drill.
  • Facilitator gives ROSC after two shocks. Team returns to the transfer, and the recorder notices whether the transfer conversation survived the arrest.
END WHEN › ROSC is announced, the transfer plan is restated, and somebody has been sent to the family.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • The EKG is read by a physician within minutes of arriving, and the activation call is made before the workup is complete.
  • The reperfusion decision is a stated comparison of two times, not a reflex.
  • The transfer plan is picked up again within a minute of ROSC, by name.
  • Debrief card prompts: who decided, and what number did they decide against? · when the rhythm changed, what got dropped?
⚠ LOCALIZATION GAPS THIS DRILL SURFACES

This drill exists mostly to test one boolean. Write down every moment somebody had to ask a question the manual should have answered.

  • caps.cathLab — the card's entire structure depends on it. Include the nights-and-weekends answer, which is often different.
  • policy.stemiActivation — who gets paged and on what number. If anybody hesitated, it is not written down well enough.
  • contacts.transfer — and whether the entry says what that destination is for.
  • defib.mfr · defib.adultJoules — surfaced by the arrest curveball. Check every cart, not just the one in the resus bay.
  • Not yet a config key: realistic ground and air transport times by hour and weather, and what is in your chest-pain bundle. Both changed the decision here.

S14Anaphylaxis → Refractory → Epinephrine Infusion

MEDICAL · 10 MIN + DEBRIEF
RECOGNITION→IM EPINEPHRINE→REFRACTORY→AIRWAY
SETUP · 1 MIN
  • Pillow patient, sitting up. Whiteboard: 34-year-old, ate at a restaurant 20 minutes ago, now covered in hives and says her throat feels tight. HR 124 · BP 92/54 · RR 24 · SpO₂ 94%.
  • Roles: leader · recorder · nurse · facilitator. Three people is enough. This is the shortest drill here and the easiest to run on a quiet night — run it first if you are trying to build the habit.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow + gown · whiteboard.
  • The whole drill in one instruction: send one person to physically fetch the epinephrine, and time them. Not the drawer it should be in — the actual vial or autoinjector, from wherever it actually is, at this hour. Then put it back unopened.
  • Point at the infusion supplies: pump, tubing, and whatever concentration this department mixes.
PHASE 1 · IM EPINEPHRINE, FAST
“She is scratching at her neck. Her voice has changed since triage. ‘It's getting harder to swallow.’”
OPEN › CODES · ANAPHYLAXIS
  • Team names anaphylaxis and gives IM epinephrine — dose, concentration, site, said out loud. The runner comes back with it and the facilitator logs the time.
  • Positioning, oxygen and IV access happen in parallel, not in sequence.
ADVANCE WHEN › IM epinephrine is given with the dose and concentration stated correctly.
PHASE 2 · CURVEBALL — IT IS NOT WORKING
“Five minutes. Second dose is in. BP 78/40, stridor at rest, she is frightened and tiring.”
  • Team escalates: fluids wide open and an epinephrine infusion — who mixes it, at what concentration, on what pump, and what is the starting rate? Say all four.
  • Somebody names the airway plan before it is needed, and says who would do it.
ADVANCE WHEN › an infusion is being prepared by a named person and an airway plan has been stated.
PHASE 3 · THE AIRWAY
“Stridor is worse. She cannot lie flat. You are going to have to take this airway, and it is going to be swollen.”
OPEN › AIRWAY · FAILED INTUBATION LADDER
  • Team states the plan out loud: who intubates, who is the second pair of hands, what the smaller tube size is, and what the front-of-neck plan is — before any drug is given.
  • Point at the surgical airway kit. Do not open it. Say who would use it.
END WHEN › a full airway plan with a named operator and a named failure plan has been said out loud.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • IM epinephrine is given in the first two minutes, in the thigh, at the right concentration — and nobody had to think about which vial.
  • The infusion is started before the airway becomes the emergency, not after.
  • The failure plan is spoken before the first attempt.
  • Debrief card prompts: how long did it take to physically get epinephrine into the room, and where was it? · did anyone reach for the wrong concentration?
⚠ LOCALIZATION GAPS THIS DRILL SURFACES

Write down every moment somebody had to ask a question the manual should have answered.

  • physical.cartDrawers — if the runner hesitated, the drawer map is wrong or too coarse.
  • physical.supraglotticSizes — named in the airway plan; check it against the packets, size by size.
  • formulary.rsiInduction — the agent for a swollen, hypotensive airway is a local decision.
  • policy.angioedema — the histaminergic-versus-bradykinin branch and the airway approach come from a local document, if you have one.
  • Not yet a config key: which epinephrine forms you stock and where, your infusion concentration and starting rate, and whether the autoinjector and the vial live in the same place. All three are worth writing into the card.

S15Pediatric DKA → Cerebral Edema

PEDS · 15 MIN + DEBRIEF
DKA→FLUIDS & INSULIN→DETERIORATION→TRANSFER
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Child-sized pillow. Whiteboard: 9-year-old, new diagnosis, vomiting for two days, breathing deeply. Glucose 34 mmol/L (610 mg/dL) · pH 7.05 · HCO₃ 6 · K⁺ 3.2. HR 138 · BP 96/60 · weight 28 kg.
  • Roles: leader · recorder (enters the weight, which drives every dose) · nurse · facilitator (voices labs and the child's mother).
  • Have a paper towel and pen ready for the fluid and insulin arithmetic. Whether the team does it on paper, in their head, or on the card is itself a finding.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: child-sized pillow · whiteboard · an empty fluid bag marked SIM.
  • Point at the pediatric weight tape and confirm the bands on it match the ones on the card. A tape that disagrees with the screen is a silent hazard.
  • Point at hypertonic saline and mannitol. Say which one is stocked, in what concentration, and where. If nobody is sure, that is the finding.
PHASE 1 · FLUIDS, INSULIN, POTASSIUM
“He is Kussmaul breathing and sleepy but rousable. Mum is terrified and asking what is happening.”
OPEN › PEDS · DIABETIC KETOACIDOSIS
  • Recorder enters the weight. Team states the fluid plan and the insulin plan with numbers — and says explicitly when insulin starts relative to fluids, and what happens to potassium first.
  • Somebody is assigned to mum with an actual sentence, not “we'll update you”.
ADVANCE WHEN › fluid rate, insulin rate and the potassium decision are all stated as numbers.
PHASE 2 · CURVEBALL — HE STOPS MAKING SENSE
“Ninety minutes in. He has a headache, he is confused, and his heart rate has dropped to 62 while his blood pressure went up. Mum says ‘he's not right.’”
  • Team names cerebral edema out loud — the bradycardia-with-hypertension pattern is the point of the case. Fluids are addressed, the head is elevated, and hyperosmolar therapy is given with a dose calculated from the weight.
  • Say which agent and which concentration, and send somebody to point at it.
ADVANCE WHEN › cerebral edema is named and a weight-based hyperosmolar dose is stated.
PHASE 3 · WHERE DOES THIS CHILD GO?
“He is a little better. He needs a pediatric ICU. It is a weekend.”
  • Team names the receiving pediatric center and places the call. Handover includes weight, the numbers, what has been given, and the neurologic change with its time.
  • Somebody asks who travels with the child and whether mum can go.
END WHEN › a pediatric destination is named, the call is placed, and transport is decided.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • The weight is entered once, early, and every dose comes off it — nobody recomputes in their head.
  • The neurologic change is named as cerebral edema within a minute, not investigated for ten.
  • Mum was spoken to twice, by the same person.
  • Debrief card prompts: where did the insulin rate come from, and did two people agree on it? · could we lay hands on hypertonic saline in under a minute?
⚠ LOCALIZATION GAPS THIS DRILL SURFACES

Write down every moment somebody had to ask a question the manual should have answered.

  • policy.pedsOrderSet — several pediatric doses come from a named local order set. Does the card cite yours?
  • physical.cartDrawers — and specifically whether the pediatric supplies are on the same cart or a different one.
  • contacts.transfer — a pediatric destination is often a different one. If your list has a single entry, it is probably incomplete.
  • Not yet a config key: your insulin infusion protocol, which hyperosmolar agent you stock and at what concentration, and whether your length-based tape matches the bands on the screen. All three deserve to be written down.

S16Massive Hematemesis → Balloon Tamponade → Transfer

MEDICAL · 15 MIN + DEBRIEF
RESUSCITATE→AIRWAY→TAMPONADE→TRANSFER
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Pillow patient. Whiteboard: 54-year-old, known cirrhosis, vomiting large volumes of blood in the ambulance bay. HR 132 · BP 82/48 · alert but grey.
  • Roles: leader · recorder · two nurses · facilitator. This case needs more hands than it looks like it does, and noticing that is part of the drill.
  • A towel soaked in red-dyed water makes this drill land in a way a whiteboard number cannot. Optional, effective, and easy to clean up.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow + gown · whiteboard · two suction canisters if you have them.
  • Find the balloon tamponade tube. Physically. This is the classic item everyone believes exists until the night it is needed. Note which tube it is, where it is, and whether the manometer is with it. Do not open it.
  • Two suction units — point at both. A single working suction is not enough for this case, and finding that out now is cheap.
  • Point at the massive transfusion cooler route: who goes, where, and how long it takes.
PHASE 1 · BLOOD, NOT CRYSTALLOID
“He fills a basin, then another. The nurse says ‘I can't get a second line.’”
OPEN › CODES · MASSIVE HEMATEMESIS
  • Team calls for blood and activates massive transfusion — saying how it is activated here, and who is being called.
  • Large-bore access is prioritized out loud. If a resuscitation line is the answer, open it and say the site.
    OPEN › PROCEDURES · RESUSCITATION LINE
  • Somebody names the drug therapy — the vasoactive agent and the antibiotic — with doses.
ADVANCE WHEN › blood is on its way, MTP is activated by the real mechanism, and access is being secured.
PHASE 2 · CURVEBALL — HE CANNOT PROTECT HIS AIRWAY
“He is drowsy now and still bleeding into his own mouth. Sat is 88 and the suction is full.”
OPEN › PROCEDURES · SALAD — CONTAMINATED AIRWAY
  • Team intubates with a contaminated-airway plan — two suctions, positioning, and who is doing what — stated before any drug.
  • Facilitator empties one suction canister mid-attempt. Somebody has to notice and fix it.
ADVANCE WHEN › the airway is secured and confirmed, with the suction problem solved by a named person.
PHASE 3 · TAMPONADE AND THE TRANSFER
“Four units in and he is still losing it. Endoscopy is not available here tonight.”
OPEN › BALLOON TAMPONADE POSTER
  • Somebody fetches the tube for real and the team walks the steps on the pillow — naming which tube it is, because the placement differs between them. Nothing is inserted and nothing is opened.
  • The transfer call happens in parallel with the tamponade, not after it. Recorder logs both times.
END WHEN › the tamponade steps have been narrated in order and the transfer is accepted.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • Blood is called for in the first minute, and nobody spends the first ten on crystalloid.
  • The airway plan accounts for the fact that the airway is full of blood, before the first attempt.
  • The team can physically produce the tamponade tube, and knows which one it is.
  • Debrief card prompts: how long did the first unit of blood actually take to arrive? · did we have two working suctions, and did we know that before we needed them?
⚠ LOCALIZATION GAPS THIS DRILL SURFACES

This drill finds equipment problems more reliably than any other. Write down every moment somebody had to ask a question the manual should have answered.

  • physical.balloonTamponadeTube — which tube, and where. If it took more than a minute to find, the location string is wrong.
  • contacts.bloodBank.how — the real activation mechanism, in the words the lab expects to hear.
  • caps.plateletsOnSite · formulary.mtpRatio — what is in the first cooler, and how long it took.
  • physical.resusRoom — and whether the kit is actually in the room the card says it is.
  • Not yet a config key: your endoscopy availability by hour, your real first-unit-of-blood time, and how many working suction units this room has. Write all three down somewhere the next team will find them.

S17Undifferentiated Overdose → Antidote → Poison Control

MEDICAL · 12 MIN + DEBRIEF
UNKNOWN INGESTION→TOXIDROME→ANTIDOTE→CONSULT
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Pillow patient. Whiteboard: 22-year-old found unresponsive with empty blister packs nearby. Nobody knows what or how much. HR 128 · BP 96/58 · RR 8 · GCS 8 · pupils mid.
  • Roles: leader · recorder · nurse · facilitator (voices the paramedic, the EKG and poison control).
  • Have a printed EKG with a wide QRS ready to hand over in phase 2.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow + gown · whiteboard · empty blister packs · a printed EKG · a real phone.
  • Walk to wherever antidotes live. Say out loud which of these are in the building right now: sodium bicarbonate, lipid emulsion, naloxone, high-dose insulin, glucagon, hydroxocobalamin. Do not open anything. Most departments discover a gap in this exercise alone.
  • Put the poison control number on the whiteboard from memory or from the card — not from a phone search. Then check it.
PHASE 1 · RESUSCITATE BEFORE YOU DIAGNOSE
“He is barely breathing. The paramedic says ‘his housemate found him, we don't know what he took.’”
OPEN › CODES · TOXICOLOGY CRASH CARD
  • Airway and breathing addressed first. Glucose checked. Naloxone considered out loud with a dose and a reason, not reflexively.
  • Team asks the paramedic the right questions: what was at the scene, what is he prescribed, who else lives there.
ADVANCE WHEN › airway is managed, glucose is known, and a toxidrome has been named or explicitly deferred.
PHASE 2 · CURVEBALL — THE QRS IS WIDE
“Here is the EKG.” (Hand over the paper.) “QRS 148 and widening. He just had a brief seizure.”
  • Team recognizes sodium-channel blockade and treats it — naming the agent, the dose, the endpoint, and who is drawing it up. Somebody is sent to fetch it for real.
  • Ventilation strategy is stated, and hyperventilation is discussed rather than assumed.
ADVANCE WHEN › the antidote is named with a dose and an endpoint, and somebody has physically located it.
PHASE 3 · POISON CONTROL AND DISPOSITION
“QRS is narrowing. Now call poison control — and be ready to tell them what you have already done.”
  • Place the call on the card's link. If the number is a placeholder, or the link does not dial, that is the finding — write it down and stop pretending.
  • The team gives a structured toxicology handover: agent suspected, amount, time, EKG, what has been given, response.
  • Disposition named, including the mental-health question — who assesses, and where does he wait?
END WHEN › poison control is called, advice is documented, and a disposition with a named owner exists.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • Resuscitation came before diagnosis, and nobody spent the first five minutes guessing the drug.
  • The wide QRS was recognized from the paper, by the team, without a prompt.
  • Poison control was called from the card, not from a search engine.
  • Debrief card prompts: which antidotes did we discover we do not stock? · how long did it take to get poison control on the phone?
⚠ LOCALIZATION GAPS THIS DRILL SURFACES

Write down every moment somebody had to ask a question the manual should have answered.

  • contacts.poisonControl.display · contacts.poisonControl.tel — and whether the link actually dialed.
  • physical.cartDrawers — where the antidotes are, drawer by drawer.
  • formulary.rsiInduction — the induction agent for a hypotensive poisoned patient.
  • Not yet a config key: your antidote stock list — which are in the building, which are a pharmacy call, and which you simply do not have. That list is the single most useful thing this drill produces, and it belongs somewhere permanent. See ONBOARDING.md, “If something is not localizable”.

S7Blunt Trauma → Tension Pneumothorax → Chest Tube → MTP

TRAUMA SET · 15 MIN + DEBRIEF
LEVEL 1 ACTIVATION→TENSION PTX→CHEST TUBE→MTP
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Pillow = a 40-year-old, high-speed MVC, 5 minutes out by EMS radio. Team assembles before arrival — that pre-brief is Phase 1. Stage next to the trauma cart; point at the chest-tube shelf.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow · whiteboard (EMS report + vitals) · SIM-labeled trauma shears · a marker X on the pillow's right chest.
  • Trauma Cart · NEEDLE DECOMPRESSION drawer: point — first move of the tension phase.
  • Trauma Cart · CHEST TUBE INSERTION / PNEUMOTHORAX TRAY / THORACOSTOMY shelves: point, don't open.
  • Blood bank: MTP number on the whiteboard; the chamber "output" is the facilitator's voice.
PHASE 1 · THE FIVE MINUTES BEFORE
"EMS radio: high-speed rollover, hypotensive on scene, intubated in the field. Five minutes out. Set your room."
OPEN › TRAUMA · LEVEL 1 ACTIVATION
  • Roles claimed out loud, activation criteria checked, blood asked about now, airway/suction checked, who's cutting clothes.
ADVANCE WHEN › roles + activation verbalized before the "patient" rolls in.
PHASE 2 · CURVEBALL — HE TENSIONS
"Two minutes into the primary survey: sat 78, BP 74/38, trachea deviated, no breath sounds on the right, neck veins full. He's intubated — pressure alarm climbing."
OPEN › TRAUMA · BLUNT POLYTRAUMA
  • Someone names it and acts: finger or needle first per the card, narrated on the pillow at the correct landmark — then commits to the tube.
ADVANCE WHEN › tension named and decompressed (narrated) at a correct site.
PHASE 3 · TUBE + BLOOD
"Air hisses out; pressure improves — then 600 mL of blood hits the chamber and keeps coming. BP dips again."
OPEN › PROCEDURES · CHEST TUBE OPEN › CODES · MTP
  • Chest tube walked on the pillow from the checklist: triangle landmarks said out loud, finger sweep, tube secured. The chamber output triggers the MTP conversation — activate it and name the transfer decision (this patient is leaving).
END WHEN › tube checklist done, MTP activated, and "he needs a trauma center" said by name.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • The room is set before the doors open. Tension is a clinical call — nobody waits for imaging. The chamber number changes the plan (blood + transfer), not just the dressing.
  • Debrief card prompt: what did we touch on the cart that we couldn't find in one reach? — feed that straight to the kit-label system.

S8Penetrating Trauma → TCA → Thoracotomy → Notification

TRAUMA SET · 18 MIN + DEBRIEF
PENETRATING CHEST→TRAUMATIC ARREST→THORACOTOMY→SPIKES→DEBRIEF
SETUP · 3 MIN
  • Pillow = a 25-year-old, single stab wound left chest, agonal on arrival. A family actor (partner) arrives mid-sim. This sim ends in a death — schedule it when there's time to debrief properly.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow with a taped X just left of the sternum · whiteboard · a quiet room + two chairs staged for the SPIKES phase.
  • Trauma Cart · NEEDLE DECOMPRESSION drawer: point — bilateral decompression is narrated first.
  • Trauma Cart · THORACOSTOMY + RIB SPREADER shelves: point, don't open — the thoracotomy is talked through with the card.
  • Family actor: briefed separately before the sim starts (they should not hear the script).
PHASE 1 · HE ARRESTS ON THE DOOR
"He had a pulse in the rig. As you move him over: nothing. Stab wound just left of the sternum."
OPEN › TRAUMA · PENETRATING TRAUMA OPEN › TRAUMATIC CARDIAC ARREST
  • The team states why TCA is not a medical code: reversible causes first — bilateral chest decompression narrated, blood in motion, the thoracotomy decision made out loud with its actual criteria (penetrating + witnessed loss = candidate).
ADVANCE WHEN › both chests decompressed (narrated) and the thoracotomy call is made with criteria.
PHASE 2 · OPEN THE CHEST
"You have the kit. Talk your way through it — incision, spread, what you're looking for, what you'd do for a hole in the heart."
OPEN › PROCEDURES · RESUSCITATIVE THORACOTOMY
  • Clamshell narrated on the pillow with the card open — landmarks, rib spreader, pericardiotomy, aortic compression. Facilitator, after a genuine effort: "the heart is empty and still. Twenty-five minutes. Nothing reversible left." The leader calls it, with the time.
ADVANCE WHEN › the procedure is narrated start-to-finish and death is pronounced with a time.
PHASE 3 · TELL HIS PARTNER
"His partner has been in the family room for ten minutes. She knows it's bad. Go tell her."
OPEN › CONVERSATIONS · SPIKES — DELIVERING BAD NEWS
  • The leader (or whoever will really do this on shift) has the conversation in-role using the card's structure — sit down, warn, say "died," stop talking. The family actor gets to be human about it.
ADVANCE WHEN › the notification happens in-role, with the word "died" used.
PHASE 4 · DEBRIEF + WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE
  • Run the Debrief card for real — this one needs it, and practicing the emotional debrief after a scripted death is the safest rehearsal there is.
  • Good looks like: reversible causes attacked before rhythm rituals, a thoracotomy decision made on criteria (either answer can be right — it must be reasoned), a pronouncement with a time, and a notification that doesn't hide behind euphemism.

S10Prolapsed Cord → Crash Section → ED Doc on Airway → Newborn

OB SET · 18 MIN + DEBRIEF
CORD PROLAPSE→CRASH SECTION→ED DOC ON AIRWAY→HANDOFF→NEONATAL RESUS
SETUP · 2 MIN
  • Pillow-mom on the stretcher. A length of IV tubing hanging out from under the gown is the cord. Towel-roll baby hidden. Whiteboard: 37 weeks · 02:10 · FHR 70.
  • Roles: ED physician (the one who will do everything) · recorder · one or two nurses · facilitator voicing mom, the fetal heart rate and OB on the phone. Deliberately understaff this one — the whole point is 02:10 on a weeknight.
  • The facilitator plays OB arriving at 9 minutes and scrubbing. OB does the operation; OB does not help with the airway or the baby.
  • Run this one after the team has done SIM 1, so the newborn half is already familiar and the new load is the role switch, not the NRP.
EQUIPMENT TO STAGE
  • Sim props: pillow-mom + gown · IV tubing as the cord · towel-roll baby · whiteboard for the clock, the FHR and who is holding what.
  • OB / Neonatal Cart · C-SECTION drawer: point, don't open — this is the drawer most people have never opened, which is the reason to run this.
  • OB / Neonatal Cart · AIRWAY / BREATHING drawer: point — infant BVM + masks for the newborn half.
  • Code Cart · airway drawers: point at the RSI kit and the front-of-neck access drawer. Priming FONA before inducing a term pregnant patient at 02:10 is the point of Phase 3.
  • Radiant warmer: turn it on for real, before the "section" starts. Warm-up time is part of the drill.
PHASE 1 · A CORD, AT 02:10
"37 weeks, membranes ruptured in the car. She walks in and the triage nurse sees a cord in the vagina. Fetal heart rate 70. You are the only physician in the building. First thirty seconds — go."
OPEN › OB & NEONATAL
  • A named person gets a hand in to elevate the presenting part and does not come out until delivery. Knee–chest or Trendelenburg, narrated.
  • OB is called — and so is everyone else in the building. At 02:10 that call is the difference between one doctor and three people.
ADVANCE WHEN › a named person is elevating, the position is changed, and the call-out has gone.
PHASE 2 · NINE MINUTES, ALONE
"Hand is in, FHR is up to 104. OB is nine minutes out and says get her ready to section. What do you do with nine minutes and no anesthetist?"
OPEN › CODES · INTUBATION / RSI CHECKLIST
  • The ED physician says out loud that they are the airway. Deciding this before the room is busy is the skill being drilled.
  • RSI checklist opened and read aloud by someone else. Warmer on. A person named for the baby — knowing it may have to be the same physician.
ADVANCE WHEN › the airway is claimed out loud, the checklist is open, and the warmer is on.
PHASE 3 · CURVEBALL — YOU ARE THE ANESTHETIST
"OB is scrubbed and waiting on you. Term pregnant, full stomach, two in the morning, and nobody to bail you out. Talk me through your induction."
OPEN › AIRWAY · DAS 2025 LADDER
  • Pre-oxygenation stated off the card. What is different about this airway is named out loud.
  • A plan B exists and the front-of-neck kit is primed before anyone gives a drug. If it is not, stop the sim and say why — this is the rewind that matters most in this case.
ADVANCE WHEN › pre-oxygenation, drugs and a stated plan B all exist before induction, and the tube is confirmed with capnography.
PHASE 4 · CURVEBALL — TWO PATIENTS, ONE DOCTOR
"Baby is out at 02:32 — floppy and blue. Mom is still intubated on the table. You are the only physician. What happens now?"
OPEN › OB · NEONATAL RESUSCITATION
  • The physician cannot be in two places, so a handover has to be spoken out loud: a named person on mom, a specific number they are watching, and an explicit trigger that brings the doctor back. "Keep an eye on her" does not count.
  • Time of birth written down. Baby to the warmer. Warm–dry–stimulate, then PPV narrated at the rate off the card — NRP, not the pediatric pathway.
  • Facilitator: if the physician walks to the baby without saying anything, freeze it and ask the room who is watching mom. Then run the phase again.
END WHEN › a named person holds mom with an explicit call-back trigger, effective PPV is running, and both patients have a stated destination.
WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE + DEBRIEF
  • The airway role is claimed early and out loud, not assumed in the moment. FONA is primed before induction because there is no second physician. The handover at delivery is a sentence with a name, a number and a trigger in it.
  • Time of birth is stamped at delivery. Somebody reports on mom without being asked.
  • Debrief card prompts: at 02:10 on a weeknight, who else is actually in this building — and did we call all of them in the first minute? · when the baby came out, what exactly was said about the mother? quote it.
  • Runs on a table as a VEMS deck when you cannot get the room or the people.
